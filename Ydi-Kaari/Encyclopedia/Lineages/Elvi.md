Elvi are the longest-lived people of Kanyiirah, regarded as the first mortal beings to walk alongside the [[Kanirûl Pantheon|Kanirûl]] and most resemble the countenance―if not the form and presence―of the Echnicans themselves. Though [[Lunatas]] herself promised all the First Children that they were still mortal, few inhabitants of the Sacred Isles today can number Elvi know to them that ended their time of walking due to natural causes. All Elvi boast natural ties to Arcane magic and are regarded as the most versatile of the Nashtaran races.

Some Elvi scholars believe that their ancestors were Echnican themselves, perhaps sharing a long lost connection to the immortal beings that came to shepherd the newborn land. Most Elvi are happy to settle anywhere beautiful, though after [[the Departure]] and subsequent wars of [[the Thinning]], the major cities of [[Cara]] and [[Duhn]] are notably siphoned of their original magic and natural beauty.

Due to their long lifespans, many Elvi that survived the wars are direct descendants of the First Children of Nashtara and remain uniquely long-lived in comparison to even their scholarly [[Shix]] or traditional [[Aqualii]] siblings. Many older Elvi feel compelled to make nostalgic pilgrimages to Delipol or to [[Ech'Yinara]], the towering, windswept seat of the [[Kanirûl Pantheon|Kanirûl]]. These compulsions also drive Elvi to deepen bonds with other creatures, as they know all too well the fleeting nature of mortal life.

# History
It is not without cause that modern scholars believe in some shared Echnican ancestry within the bloodlines of the Elvi: the stories tell of how the Diur'eli and Nox'eli were favored by Solanas and Lunatas, respectively.

# Traits
Your Elvi character has the following hereditary traits.
**Age**. Although Elvi reach physical maturity around age 20, mental and emotional faculties reach full maturity around the age of 100. %% Diur'eli and Nox'eli can live to be 550 years old, while the unique characteristics of the Ganzi reincarnation cycle do not yet have an observed age limitation. %%
**Size**. Your size is Medium. Elvi range from under 5 feet to over 6 feet tall and tend toward slender builds, averaging only a little over 100 pounds.
**Speed**. You have a walking speed of 30 feet.
**Magic Ancestry**. You have advantage on saves against being charmed, and magic can’t put you to sleep. Additionally, you have proficiency in Arcana.
**Trance**. Elvi have strangely never needed to succumb to sleep. Instead, they enter a meditative trance state, remaining semiconscious for 4 hours a day. You choose whether or not you dream while meditating. After resting in this way, you gain the same benefit that other creatures do from 8 hours of sleep.
**Attuned Prescience**. You gain one of the following traits determined by the inherited characteristics of your character:
- ***Inheritor of the Suhns***. As a Diur'eli you can cast the lowest circle version of the _comprehend languages_ spell as an action without using a spell slot. You can use this trait once per rest. Your spellcasting ability for this spell is WIS, INT, or CHA (choose which during character creation). Additionally, you have proficiency in Persuasion. When you finish a long rest, you can choose to exchange this proficiency for Insight, and vice versa.
- ***Inheritor of the Moons***. As a Nox'eli, when you are in darkness, dim light, or a shadow large enough to cover your body, you can cast the _misty step_ spell as an action without using a spell slot. You can use this trait once per rest. Your spellcasting ability for this spell is WIS, INT, or CHA (choose which during character creation). Additionally, you gain darkvision out to a range of 60 feet.

# Cultural Heritages
## Diur'eli
The Diur'eli, known as the Children of Daylight and Inheritors of the Suhns, were blessed by Solanas to be bearers of his tenants and to shine as natural leaders amongst the Nashtarans. They bear skin tones ranging from deep browns to mild tans, and eyes of brilliant white, amber, or golden hues. Their hair colors can range from black to stark white, and are often braided
Living boisterous, energetic lives, they are often individualistic, ambitious, and high-aspiring socialites.
#### Origins
When Solanas first walked among the Nashtarans, he sought those who rose earliest to greet the dawn—merchants preparing their stalls, guards beginning their watch, craftsmen kindling their forges. These industrious souls drew his attention through their eagerness to seize each day's potential. Impressed by their dedication, the Dawnfather marked them with features that would attract and voices that carried his own commanding resonance of divine authority. Some say Tympestyr also touched these chosen, blessing the boldest among them with silver tongues for trade and negotiation.

#### Society and Culture
The Diur'eli thrive in the bustle of daylight hours, forming the backbone of urban commerce and governance across Cara-Duhn's major cities. Their culture celebrates achievement, innovation, and the forming of bonds—viewing social webs as the crucial threads that weave society together. Natural orators and negotiators, many Diur'eli serve as guild masters, diplomatic envoys, and shrewd merchants, with their ability to read subtle social cues and hold the multitude of Cara-Duhnian dialects in their back pocket making them invaluable in positions of competing interests.

Their most honored tradition is the **Suhiir'val**, or "Sunwright's Calling," which in Ropesh has evolved into something uniquely powerful. Young Diur'eli must first pass the Dynasty's merit examinations like any citizen, but their Suhiir'val goes beyond mere testing—they must identify a critical gap in their community's web of [[Ropeshian Dynasty#Oaths of the Layman|oaths]] and create an entirely new sworn position to fill it.

Where traditional Ropeshian oaths maintain existing order, the Suhiir'val _expands_ it. A successful Diur'eli might establish the office of "Grain Mediator" to prevent disputes between farmers and millers, or create the role of "Dawn Witness" to formally record and verify the oaths of others at first light. The most ambitious might even convince multiple towns to adopt their innovation, spreading their new oath-bound position across the Dynasty.

This practice has made the Diur'eli the architects of Ropesh's increasingly complex bureaucracy—every generation adding new layers of sworn positions that, while genuinely useful, also create opportunities for Diur'eli administration. Success in one's Suhiir'val is measured not just by passing the merit examinations, but by how many communities adopt your created oath and how long it endures. The most successful become **Rex'ora**, "Golden Speakers," whose invented positions have become so essential that removing them would collapse entire sectors of governance.

The cynical note that Ropesh's bureaucracy grows ever more labyrinthine precisely because each generation of Diur'eli must create something new to prove themselves—yet even critics admit these additions usually address real problems that the traditional oath system overlooked.

Unlike their Nox'eli cousins who embrace contemplation, the Diur'eli philosophy centers on _manifestation through action_. They believe the Suhns' daily journey teaches that rest is temporary but achievement is eternal—carved in stone, forged in metal, or woven into the social fabric. This drives their famous work ethic but also their notorious tendency toward pride and reluctance to admit failure.

The Diur'eli gravitate toward two primary religious expressions:

- **Vólat-rah Orthodox**: Many embrace the new orthodoxy's emphasis on striving for perfection and transformation, seeing it as divine mandate to reshape the world according to Solanas's vision of order and prosperity.
- **Mercantile Syncretism**: Others blend worship pragmatically, honoring Solanas at dawn, Pyronia at their work, and even offering quiet prayers to Lunatas when contracts must be signed under her witness.

#### Relations with Others

The Diur'eli's natural charisma and networking ability make them welcome in most societies, though their ambition sometimes breeds resentment. They work particularly well with industrious **Oread**, innovative **House Tarahn** nobles, and surprisingly, find kindred spirits in **Aqualii** merchants who share their appreciation for negotiation and profit.

Their relationship with the Nox'eli remains complex but complementary—while some see them as opposite sides of the same coin, wise leaders recognize their need for each other. Diur'eli often employ Nox'eli astronomers to time their ventures by favorable stars, while Nox'eli rely on Diur'eli merchants to distribute their rare nocturnal goods. This symbiosis is celebrated during the eclipses, when both communities unite in grand festivals that honor both sun and moon.

> [!recite|bg-c-yellow] _"Each dawn we rise not to merely witness the light, but to carry it forward—in our works, our words, our bonds with others. We are not blessed by the Suhns to hoard their radiance, but to kindle prosperity in every shadow we touch."_ ~ Diur'eli merchant's morning prayer

## Nox'eli
The Nox'eli, known as the Children of Twilight and Inheritors of the Moons, were blessed by Lunatas and even subtly touched by Vespera. These nocturnal beings bear pale, light skin. Their natural hair colors flow between blonde or dark browns. Their eyes are well adjusted to the dim light of the moons, sporting hues of luminous silver, red, or amethyst irises. 
They live quiet, introspective lives centered around core communities, often dedicated to the cultivation of rare night-blooming plants or the pursuits of philosophical exploration or astronomical sciences.

#### Origins
In ancient times, the first Nox'eli faithfully tended the sacred night-gardens dedicated to [[Lunatas]], cultivating rare flora that bloomed only under [[Moons#Roida|Roida]]’s crimson gaze or [[Moons#Valya|Valya]]’s gentle luminance. When their devotion attracted Lunatas, she marked them as hers, blessing them with eyes attuned perfectly to moonlight, granting them clarity to fully embrace the serenity of night. [[Darkness|Vespera]], also intrigued by their reverence for subtlety and secrecy, bestowed upon them the gift of silent footsteps and shadow-weaving. Yet, even amidst these profound gifts, the Nox'eli often found themselves gazing skyward, entranced by the shimmering mysteries of the stars, subtly influenced by the mother [[Fate|Asteria]]

#### Society and Culture
Scattered throughout Cara-Duhn, the Nox'eli thrive during twilight and midnight hours, dwelling primarily in small, secluded communities within the grander settlements and cities of the day-walking Cara-Duhnians. Their nocturnal lifestyle complements their contemplative nature, creating a culture that values quiet observation, deep meditation, and subtle interaction with the unseen world. Masters of nocturnal agriculture, the Nox'eli nurture night-blooming flora and fauna renowned for their magical properties, medicinal qualities, and symbolic significance.

Their natural affinity for stargazing and celestial cycles draws them especially toward doctrines influenced by Mother Asteria, further deepening their scholarly and prophetic pursuits. Revered scholars known as the Vox'li, or "Starkeepers", meticulously document celestial movements, interpreting them through complex astrological systems attributed to Asteria’s teachings. When not bent on their studies, Nox of this employment will often take on the role of weavers, crafting textiles shimmering subtly with starlight threads collected from their moonlit gardens.

Another calling that young Nox often are encouraged to pursue at some point in their life is the flowing art of Moonspeaking. These arts were first taught by Lunatas and later began incorporating the natural abilities granted them by Vespera. Harmonizing with the movements of Kanyiirah through their voices and embodying the unique rhythms of the sister moons in their dance across the night sky, these Vix'lo, or "Moonspeakers", elevate their consciousness beyond this current reality. The sacred ritual allows a Nox to momentarily touch the [[Planes of Existence|Ethereal]], weaving illusion magics and subtle enchantments to commune with the Greater Spirits in ways only the high druids have ever achieved.

The Nox'eli embody a philosophy balancing the mystical and tangible worlds, integrating the wise teachings of Lunatas, the subtle secrets of Vespera, and the cosmic insights inspired by Asteria. Their beliefs often draw them to two primary religious factions:
- [[Vólat Church#Current|Vólat-sen]]: Valuing tradition, these adherents harmonize their worship of both pantheons, embracing old rites and venerating ancestral saints, seeing continuity and balance as sacred.
- [[Doctrines#Kindleheart Concordia|Kindleheart Concordia]]: Some Nox'eli gravitate toward this doctrine to pursue freedom in the intricate weavings of the constellations. They are respected as mediators, spiritual guides, and interpreters, offering impartial counsel to explorers and those wrestling with their place in the wider world.

#### Relations with others
The Nox'eli, although self-contained, remain peacefully neutral, willing to guide outsiders or share insights when respectfully approached. Their impartial stance and diplomatic wisdom have earned them reverence as arbiters during disputes among Cara-Duhn’s peoples. They can often be found congregating with [[Shix]], noble [[Alseid]], and older [[Elvi#Ganzi|Ganzi Elvi]].
While misunderstood by some, those who interact with Nox often find them gentle, scholarly, and wise allies rather than elusive or hostile neighbors.
