# Details
**Demonym**: Ropeshian 
**Neighbors**:
- [[Carain Empire]]
- [[Tashi]] river clans
**Cities**:
- [[Sungard]]
- [[Oppao]]
**Known for**:
- Grains, livestock, and spices

An enigmatic and versatile culture that values work ethic, familial honor, personal growth, and martial prowess. Their wide, windswept foothills have been the proving ground for some of the greatest heroes ever seen in Cara-Duhnian history.

The **Khan-Yar** is the ceremonial sovereign, descended from the original Ropesh bloodline. They reside in the Palace of First Light within Sungard's inner sanctum, performing ritualistic duties tied to the harvest cycles, blessing new oaths, and serving as the spiritual embodiment of Ropeshian unity. Since the Thinning, they have been sequestered from true governance, kept comfortable but impotent and only have their authority invoked during extreme moments of unrest.
They are a symbol traditionalists cling to, especially important to the rural layman that make up the main population of the Ropeshian fiefdoms.

The **Khagan** is the true elected emperor of the Dynasty, chosen from the ranks of the Khagan-Khans once every 15 years, or following a death of the previous Khagan.

Under the rule of the Khagan are several **Khagan-Khan** governors that each hold a territorial **Khanate** of the Dynasty's land, maintaining order, collecting taxes, and training regional levies. They compete viciously for influence and wealth, knowing the next Khagan will be chosen from their ranks.

Regional **Khal-Vyr** officials handle the day-to-day within the cities and towns of the Khanate, acting as traveling judges and settling mayoral disputes that are brought to them by the Vólatian Rectors and Prelates that act as community leaders. 

## Oaths of the layman
A certain quirk of Ropeshian culture is that each member of society takes an oath to uphold their responsibilities for their community: the baker swears to bake to a certain standard, the captain of the guard swears to keep his men fit and capable of defending the walls, the judge swears to execute the spirit of their Khagan-Khan's law, the blacksmith swears to mend and upkeep the tools of the townspeople to the highest craftmanship, the priest swears to council with truth and seek the favor of the gods, etc.

## History
An alliance of marriage between the two great family lineages of Ki’ju Rrop and Zintzu Pesh birthed the mighty and honor-bound nation of Ropesh. It was said that Zintzu Pesh was the offspring of [[Taerannon]] himself, her mother having caught the eye of The Untamed as an acolyte in the Silver Hills. She was a fair beauty of gentle spirit, promised in marriage to the great Pheonix-slayer himself.

Due to unforeseen illness of the blood though, the original Ropesh family quicky died out within 40 years. Desperate for leadership, the feudal lords and cousins of the Pesh line elected a new Ropeshian **Khagan**, or "honor-bearer" from their own ranks to assume the family title, and continue to pick from these noble houses every few generations to maintain order within the nation. During [[the Thinning]], the Ropeshian Dynasty floundered in seclusion under the feudal rule of Sir Mokoda Scottsdale I, who was himself a [[_Lineages#Post-Nashtaran Emergences|secret descendant]] of [[Fate of the Heartkindler|Rathus]].

# Current Status
In the gilded capital of Sungard, several Diur'eli families have effectively monopolized the administrative apparatus beneath the Khagan's court. While the Khagan-Khan nobles jostle for political favor and military commands, the mercantile bureaus are the backbone that quietly ensure the Dynasty actually _functions_. They manage the grain tallies, coordinate livestock distributions, oversee the new irrigation projects, and—most critically—serve as trusted intermediaries between the increasingly distant Khagan and his restless subjects, even as they pad the pockets of the Khal-Vyrs just well enough to keep them comfortable and turn a deaf ear to the grumblings of their laboring subjects.

Still prosperous, but fraying at the edges, the Ropeshians have readily adopted the new Volat-rah orthodoxy, though some traditionalists still cling to the outlawed Volat-sin beliefs in secret. Peasants face high taxes, labor shortages, and economic pressures from the [[Carain Empire]], so reform of any kind is gladly accepted among the masses.

The living Khan-Yar, Tesao Ropesh IV, is a frail, elderly figure more interested in poetry and astronomy than the current state of the nation. His health is carefully watched by the mercantile bureaus, who fear what chaos might erupt should he die during such volatile times within the Vólat Church.

The current Khagan, Yamao Ropesh, is also the youngest brother of the Khan-Yar and a stern man who once crushed all dissents and rebels across the domain of his Khanate. Plots swirl in the golden-domed capital of [[Sungard]] as the nobles and military industrialists vie for power and influence over the aging emperor. Though the Khagan rules absolutely from his gilded palace, he has grown distant from the needs of the people and is increasingly plagued by the perceived threats of the Empire in the west. He has been delegating most affairs to his chief councilor Vros Malo while the real power—his ability to command the Khans—slowly erodes.

One of the Khagan's chief councilors, Vros Malo, handles many of the bureaucratic affairs of state now, growing rich off bribes and corruption of the lesser lords of the outer regions. He secretly plots to eliminate the Khagan's sponsored and preferred replacement, Shogun Lako, and to seize power himself once Yamao is displaced.

While the common folk work the vast Ropeshian fields and peasants see after the herds of livestock that graze the rolling southern hills, skilled engineers are currently constructing a new network of roads and irrigation canals across the kingdom, bought and paid for by the mercantile bureaus of Sungard. However, peasant revolts are on the rise, as the people are starting to feel the burden of an absent-minded Khagan coupled with the money-hungry maelstrom of the trade guilds.
